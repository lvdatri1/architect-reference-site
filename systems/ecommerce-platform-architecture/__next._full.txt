## What this system is

An e-commerce platform is the **commerce loop**: how a shopper on a website becomes an order that ships, gets paid for, and posts to the books. The architecture splits into a **storefront** (fast, read-heavy, customer-facing) and a **back office** (consistent, write-heavy, inventory-and-ledger-facing). The seam between them — and between the platform and the [ERP](../enterprise-resource-planning) — is where e-commerce architectures live or die.

The classic shape is orders-in, inventory-and-cash-out. Most of the interesting engineering is exactly in the step a store demo never shows: **checkout is easy when nothing goes wrong, and everything is about what happens when an order is out of stock, fails payment, or gets returned.**

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    B[Browse / catalog] --> C[Cart]
    C --> K[Checkout + payment]
    K --> O[Order]
    O --> F[Fulfillment / warehouse]
    O --> L[Ledger posting]
    F -->|tracking / delivery| D[Delivered]
    D -.->|returns| R[Returns + refunds]
```

## The commerce loop, step by step

| Stage | System of record | Failure mode to design for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Browse + search | Catalog / search engine | Data freshness of price and stock |
| Cart | Short-lived session or saved cart | Abandonment, price change mid-session |
| Checkout | Checkout service | The user leaves; the order must not be double-charged |
| Payment | Payment gateway / PSP | 3DS, declined card, authorization expiry |
| Order capture | OMS | Conflicting orders, oversell, address validation |
| Fulfillment | WMS + carriers | Split shipments, backorders, lost parcels |
| Post-sale | Returns / refunds | RMA approval, restock, GL correction |
| Ledger | ERP finance | Reconciliation of gateway settlements and refunds |

## Storefront vs back office

| Concern | Storefront side | Back office side |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reads | Millions of page views, cached | Few, precise |
| Writes | Cart and orders | Inventory, ledgers, shipments |
| Consistency | Eventually consistent is fine | Strongly consistent is mandatory |
| Latency | Milliseconds matter | Minutes are acceptable |
| Source of truth winner | The catalog cache | The OMS + ERP |

The rule: **never let the storefront read inventory straight from the WMS on every page view**. Cache the promotable view of the catalog and stock (with the stocks levels in the OMS), and reserve inventory at order capture, not at page render.

## Checkout to order to money

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    CHECKOUT["Checkout<br/>address + payment"] -->|authorize| AUTH["Payment intent<br/>authorize"]
    AUTH -->|reserve + confirm| ORDER["Order created<br/>OMS reserves stock"]
    ORDER -->|capture at ship| CAPTURE["Capture<br/>money moves"]
    ORDER -->|fulfill| SHIP["Ship + tracking"]
    CAPTURE -->|settlement feed| ERP["ERP posting<br/>revenue + COGS"]
    SHIP -->|COGS posting| ERP
```

## Payment basics every architect should know

- **Authorization vs capture**: authorize at checkout, capture when you ship. Authorization expires (typically 3–7 days), so delayed shipping needs re-authorization — a classic cause of failed orders.
- **3D Secure**: the bank's step-up that shifts chargeback liability; the conversion hit is real, and it must be a configuration, not an afterthought.
- **Settlement reconciliation**: gateway reports money moved in *batches*; the ERP sees per-order revenue. Every order, fee, refund, and payout needs to tie out line-by-line — this loop is the hardest to automate and the first thing an auditor checks.
- **Refunds**: money sent back is *not* the reverse of a capture; it is a separate movement with its own fees and timings.

## When to build vs buy

Buy the platform when commerce is core but not the differentiator and you want the upgrade path, hosting, and payment connectors handled. Build (or aggressively extend) when your business model depends on custom pricing, selling experiences, or fulfillment logic that no package models — but then you have signed up to own payments security, PSD2/PCA-DSS-adjacent compliance, and every integration. Most realistic answer for the long tail: **a headless storefront on a commerce core**, custom only at the edges you actually differentiate on.

## Tester checklist

- Double-payment: refresh the pay page, re-click pay with a lagging gateway — exactly one charge and one order, never two.
- Stock race: two customers buy the last unit concurrently — one order reserves it, the other backorders cleanly, and the WMS never ships more than it has.
- Auth expiry: an order authorized but not shipped before the authorization expiry re-authenticates instead of silently failing.
- Reconciliation: for a synthetic day of orders, refunds, and fees, the gateway settlement report ties to the ERP GL within tolerance.
- Checkout data: price and stock shown at the cart must be validated at the order level (promo codes, coupons, address validation).
- Returns loop: an approved return reverses inventory and posts the correct GL correction, net of restocking/fees.
- Catalog freshness: changing a price in the catalog reflects in the cache with bounded latency, and the checkout revalidates the price before capture.

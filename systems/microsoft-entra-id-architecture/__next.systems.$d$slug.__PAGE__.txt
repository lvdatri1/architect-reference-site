## What this system is

Microsoft Entra ID (formerly Azure Active Directory) is Microsoft's **cloud identity provider**: the directory of users, devices, and apps behind Microsoft 365, Azure, and thousands of SaaS apps. It is the identity backbone of most modern Windows/Office estates, and the mental model that unlocks it is **tenant**: every organization gets an isolated tenant (a directory of identities + the policies that govern them), and everything else — apps, SaaS, devices, external collaborators — hangs off that one boundary. "Entra ID architecture" is mostly the *art of deciding what a tenant is* and how identities flow across trust boundaries.

```mermaid
flowchart TB
  subgraph Tenant["Your tenant (the trust boundary)"]
    U[Internal users]
    D[Devices - enrolled]
    A1[Apps - OIDC / SAML / WS-Fed]
    SAAS[Connected SaaS - 2000+ gallery apps]
    POL[Conditional Access + policies]
    RB[RBAC - roles and scopes]
  end
  subgraph External["External identities"]
    B2B[Guest users - B2B]
    B2C[Consumer identities - B2C tenant]
    FED[Federated orgs - cross-tenant]
  end
  External --> Tenant
```

## The core objects that make the directory

| Object | What it is | The failure if ignored |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tenant** | One isolated directory (a `*.onmicrosoft.com` domain), the security boundary | Two orgs merged into one tenant = one boundary confusion |
| **User** | Directory principal with identity + attributes + entitlements | Guest/vendor users dumped into the same object model as employees |
| **Group** | Member container used for assignment | Assigning apps directly to users instead of groups = unmanageable sprawl |
| **Service principal / enterprise app** | The *instance* of an app in your tenant (the "who" that gets permissions) | Confusing the app registration (the definition) with the principal (the grants) |
| **Device identity** | Enrolled devices with their own identity for [zero trust](../zero-trust-architecture) | Devices trusted by account only — posture checks have nothing to attach to |
| **App registration** | The app's metadata: client ID, redirects, API scopes | Client secrets committed to repos; wrong redirect URIs |

The three that trip everyone: **tenant** (the boundary), **app registration vs service principal** (definition vs instance), and **group-based assignment** (the only thing that scales). Get those three right and most Entra architecture is sound.

## The identity lifecycle: join, access, leave

```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
  participant HR as HR / source of truth
  participant E as Entra ID
  participant Apps as Apps + SaaS
  participant P as Conditional Access

  HR->>E: provision user (joiner)
  E->>Apps: assign via group + provision (SCIM)
  E->>P: device enrolls, posture established
  P->>Apps: grant sign-in when compliant
  HR->>E: offboard (leaver)
  E->>E: disable, revoke sessions, block sign-in
  E->>Apps: deprovision access (SCIM)
```

Users are born from an HR/source-of-truth, assigned to groups, provisioned into SaaS via [SCIM](../scim-identity-provisioning), and on leaving are disabled, session-revoked, and deprovisioned. The loop is the same as [joiner-mover-leaver](../joiner-mover-leaver) everywhere; Entra's twist is that **Conditional Access evaluates posture at sign-in**, so the directory is also an enforcement point, not just a list.

## External identities: B2B, B2C, and cross-tenant

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  subgraph Ext["External identity - in YOUR tenant"]
    G[Guest user<br/>- has an account elsewhere]
    GC[Guest account -<br/>a shadow object in your directory]
  end
  G -->|invited / redempt| GC
  GC -->|sign-in via their own IdP| Apps[Your apps]
  B2C[Consumer tenant<br/>- separate directory for public users] --> C[Customer-facing apps]
```

- **B2B collaboration** is for *business collaborators*: you invite an external user (who keeps their home identity) into your tenant as a **guest**. Entra stores a shadow guest object, but sign-in happens at the guest's own IdP — you never manage their password. Guests then get app access like anyone, scoped through Conditional Access. This is the model for vendors, partners, and contractors.
- **B2C** (Azure AD B2C) is the opposite: a *separate consumer tenant* where you create and own end-user identities for your public/customer-facing apps (social sign-in, self-service registration). It is **not** the same tenant as your employees — that split is deliberate and mandatory for governance.
- **Cross-tenant access** is when two orgs federate or trust each other's tenants (M365 shared channels, guest sync via [SCIM](../scim-identity-provisioning)); Entra now manages this with cross-tenant access settings and an external-collaboration policy that whitelists/blacklists which tenants can be invited.

The B2B vs B2C decision in one line: **B2B = "your vendor can see our SharePoint," B2C = "our customers have accounts in our app."** Mixing consumer and employee identity in one directory is the classic governance disaster — which is exactly why B2C is its own tenant type.

## Tenant topology: the decision that defines the architecture

```mermaid
flowchart TD
  Q[One tenant or many?] --> C{Regulatory / legal separation required?}
  C -->|yes - hard isolation| M[Multiple tenants with<br/>cross-tenant rules]
  C -->|no| S{Single operational unit?}
  S -->|yes| ONE[Single tenant - default, simplest]
  S -->|no - brands / subsidiaries| SEG[Segmented tenants -<br/>cost of cross-tenant sharing]
```

| Decision | Choose when | The price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Single tenant** | One org, one governance model, default for most | Everything shares one boundary; guest bloat if partners pile in |
| **Multiple tenants** | Regulatory isolation, subsidiaries, B2C vs employee | Cross-tenant sharing (guests, channels, sync) is real ongoing cost |
| **B2C as its own tenant** | Any consumer-facing app | Must consciously keep employee and consumer identity apart |

The architecture mistake to avoid: **letting tenant topology grow by accident.** Every "just invite them into our tenant" adds a guest; every "can we sync their staff?" adds a cross-tenant rule; before long the boundary is ambiguous. Decide the topology deliberately, apply an external-collaboration policy, and treat tenant boundaries as the [defense-in-depth](../defense-in-depth) perimeter they are.

## Conditional Access: the enforcement engine

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  Sign[Sign-in attempt] --> CA{Every policy evaluated}
  CA -->|block / require MFA / require compliant device| P[Allowed only if posture + context OK]
  CA -->|session controls| SS[Session - app session / sign-in frequency]
```

Conditional Access is where Entra becomes zero-trust enforcement: *every* sign-in is evaluated against policies that combine who (user/group), what (app), where (location), and *device posture*. The signature policies: **require MFA for admins**, **require a compliant device**, **block legacy authentication**, **location-based risk**, and **sign-in frequency/session controls**. It is the mechanism that makes "trusted device + MFA + right location" a requirement instead of a wish, and it's why [endpoint security](../endpoint-security-architecture) and Entra are one system, not two.

## The failure modes an architect owns

- **Tenant sprawl**: a tenant per pilot, per team, per "we might need it" — cross-tenant sharing becomes ungovernable.
- **Mixing B2B, B2C, and employees**: guests and customers and staff in one directory with one policy surface — the governance disaster B2C's separate tenant exists to prevent.
- **Assigning apps directly to users**: no groups, so every app is a user-by-user list; onboarding/offboarding means touching hundreds of rows.
- **No Conditional Access**: sign-in works from any device, any location, no MFA-for-admins — the estate runs on trust and passwords.
- **Legacy auth left on**: protocols that bypass MFA (Basic auth, IMAP/SMTP) still enabled, silently defeating every policy.
- **App registration/principal confusion**: a developer's app registered with broad delegated permissions that outlive its purpose; scopes never trimmed.
- **Guest bloat**: external collaborators invited into the employee tenant with no lifecycle policy, no expiry, no review.

## Tester checklist

- **Guest lifecycle**: invite an external user, confirm their sign-in happens at their own IdP, that they can reach only what's assigned, and that removing them from the group immediately blocks access.
- **B2B vs B2C boundary**: confirm consumer identities live in a separate B2C tenant and can never resolve into the employee directory.
- **Conditional Access realness**: craft a sign-in from a disallowed location/device and confirm the policy *blocks or requires MFA*, not merely logs.
- **MFA for admins**: a global-admin sign-in without MFA must be impossible in test — the policy applies even from a "trusted" device.
- **SCIM provisioning**: change a user's group membership and confirm the downstream SaaS app's entitlements sync within the SLA; on offboarding, deprovisioning lands.
- **Tenant topology audit**: list all tenants, guests, and cross-tenant rules and confirm each is deliberate and covered by policy — no accidental boundaries.
- **App principal hygiene**: enumerate service principal permissions and confirm none exceed their current purpose (least privilege, scopes trimmed).

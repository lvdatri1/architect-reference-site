## What this system is

A **quantum computer** computes with **qubits** instead of bits. Where a classical bit is 0 or 1, a qubit is a *superposition* — a probability mixture of 0 and 1 — and two or more qubits can be **entangled**, meaning their states are correlated in ways that have no classical analog. Programs are written as **quantum circuits**: qubits prepared, gates applied, then **measured** back into classical bits.

That is the entire physics, and the hard part is that measurement *destroys the state*: you do not "read" intermediate answers, you run a quantum circuit many times and collect the statistical *distributions* of the outputs. The famous victories (Shor for factoring, Grover for search) exist because those distributions concentrate disproportionately on the right answer with exponentially fewer operations than any classical algorithm.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    Q[Qubits - superposition + entanglement] --> C[Quantum circuit - gates]
    C --> M[Measure - collapse to bits]
    M --> R[Run N times - distribution]
    R --> D[Extract answer statistically]
    Q --> E[Error correction - protects qubits]
```

## Thinking in quantum instead of bits

| Classical bit | Qubit |
| --- | --- |
| Always 0 or 1 | Superposition — a probability over both |
| Many bits = one state of the register | N qubits = 2^N amplitudes in a single state vector |
| Read the answer directly | Measuring collapses the state; you sample the distribution |
| Programs are IF/ELSE | Programs are unitary gates — reversible, no "assignments" |
| Errors are rare and local | Qubits decohere easily; error correction consumes massive overhead |

The mental model for an architect: a quantum program is **not a faster version of a loop** — it is a *different calculus* that only wins on specific problems (factoring, optimization heuristics, quantum chemistry, some simulations). For everything else, a classical box is still better and cheaper.

## The stack today

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    USER["Problem<br/>optimization, chemistry, search"] -->|formulate| ALG["Quantum algorithm<br/>Shor, Grover, VQE, QAOA"]
    ALG -->|circuit| CIR["Circuit compiler<br/>optimizes + maps to hardware"]
    CIR -->|run| HW["Quantum hardware<br/>superconducting / trapped-ion / photonic"]
    CIR -->|map to gate set| CLASS["Classical control + error correction"]
    CLASS -->|compile to gates| HW
    HW -->|qubits measured| OUT["Statistical result<br/>hybrid classical post-processing"]
```

The real architectures are **hybrid**: a classical machine prepares and reads the quantum processor, error-corrects it, and post-processes the samples. Nobody runs a whole business on qubits; the practical pattern today is the quantum processor as an accelerator for a narrow, mathematically hard subroutine called from a classical workflow.

## Where quantum genuinely wins

| Problem domain | Quantum advantage | Classical fallback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Factoring large integers (Shor) | Exponential speedup — the basis of [post-quantum crypto](../secrets-certificates-and-pki) fears | RSA/ECC breaks as qubit count grows; hence **PQ crypto migration** |
| Unstructured search (Grover) | Quadratic speedup — square root of classical steps | Rarely decides the world; quadratic is no free lunch |
| Quantum chemistry / materials | Natural fit — electrons *are* quantum | Best classical approximations only |
| Combinatorial optimization (QAOA/VQE) | Useful heuristics, but no proven speedup over classical heuristics today | Validates against classical solvers |

The honest scoreboard: **the "quantum advantage" is proven for very few problems, and almost all current machines are narrow, error-prone, and (post-error-correction) slow.** Practical enterprises should plan for *two* realities: keep optimizing classically now, and prepare for the crypto threat (harvest-decrypt: encrypted-now, decrypted-later).

## Tester checklist

- Superposition sanity: a 1-qubit circuit in a balanced superposition returns ~50/50 over many shots, within statistical tolerance — not all 0s or all 1s.
- Gate determinism: the same circuit returns the same distribution when run repeatedly on the same simulator/hardware device within noise budget.
- Measurement collapse: reading a qubit once yields a definite 0 or 1, and re-running masks the state — no "peeking" between gates.
- Error-correction cost: confirm the physical-to-logical qubit overhead and the resulting latency on real hardware versus simulator.
- Hybrid integration: a classical workflow calls the quantum library, parses the returned distribution, and reaches the documented decision path — with a classical fallback when the provider is down.
- Crypto migration planning: inventory which systems depend on RSA/ECC and the harvest-decrypt exposure they carry for the [post-quantum shift](../secrets-certificates-and-pki).
- Noise accounting: results are reported with shot counts and error/readout metrics, and the analysis accounts for those error rates before drawing conclusions.

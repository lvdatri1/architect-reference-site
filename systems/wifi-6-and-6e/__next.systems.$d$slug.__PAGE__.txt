## What this system is

Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax) is the first standard written for **dense, crowded networks** rather than fatter megabytes. Wi-Fi 5 was "make one client fast." Wi-Fi 6 is "make 50 clients tolerable" — which is why the headline features are OFDMA, MU-MIMO, and Target Wake Time, not raw Mbps.

Wi-Fi 6E adds the **6 GHz band**, an entirely new, uncrowded set of channels that only 6E-and-newer devices can use. It's the same protocol on new spectrum — the win is *exclusivity*, not just the protocol.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  AP["Wi-Fi 6 AP<br/>OFDMA + MU-MIMO"] -- "MU-MIMO spatial" --> C1["Client 1<br/>low packet"]
  AP -- "OFDMA RU" --> C2["Client 2<br/>streaming"]
  AP -- "small RU" --> C3["Client 3<br/>sensor"]
  C1 -- uplink --> CH["One frame, shared by<br/>resource units (OFDMA)"]
  C2 -- uplink --> CH
  C3 -- uplink --> CH
  CH -- "fewer waits" --> OK["All served in parallel"]
```

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  AP[Wi-Fi 6 AP] -->|OFDMA: one frame split into resource units| C1[Client 1 small packet]
  AP -->|OFDMA: resource unit| C2[Client 2 streaming]
  AP -->|OFDMA: tiny resource unit| C3[Client 3 sensor]
  AP -->|MU-MIMO: 8 spatial streams| C4[Multiple antennas at once]
```

## What actually changed with Wi-Fi 6

| Feature | What it does | The pain it fixes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **OFDMA** | Splits one frame into resource units, serving several clients simultaneously | "low-data clients hog the medium" — back-to-back small packets become one parallel burst |
| **MU-MIMO (8 streams)** | Transmits to several clients on separate spatial streams at once | Wi-Fi 5 did downlink MU-MIMO poorly; Wi-Fi 6 makes it real and adds uplink |
| **Target Wake Time (TWT)** | Devices tell the AP when they'll wake; AP schedules sleep | **Battery life** — IoT and low-power devices stop polling endlessly |
| **BSS coloring** | Frames from crowding APs get a "color" and are ignored instead of causing backoff | The "dense apartment Wi-Fi" slowdown — repeaters among neighbors stop screaming |
| **1024-QAM** | More bits per symbol at high SNR | Bling for fast, close, clean links |

The counterintuitive truth: Wi-Fi 6 doesn't make a single laptop feel 2x faster on a quiet network. It makes 30 laptops not-crawl on a busy one. If you're alone on a channel, Wi-Fi 5 with wide channel is still very fast; Wi-Fi 6 pays off where the network is *populated*.

## Why 6 GHz (6E) is the real story

```mermaid
flowchart TB
  OLD[2.4 + 5 GHz<br/>crowded, shared with legacy] --> NOW[6 GHz
  ]
  NOW --> A[Only 6E+ devices can join]
  NOW --> B[Many wide, clean 80/160 MHz channels]
  NOW --> C[No DFS radar weathership timeouts]
  NOW --> D[Same Wi-Fi 6 protocol, new spectrum]
  NOW --> Q[The highway is empty again]
```

The decisive changes: **new clients only** (the band forces capability), **no DFS** (no radar-detection pauses), wide 80/160 MHz channels without fighting neighbors for them, and 6E mandates WPA3 — no legacy-weakness mode. If you're upgrading a fleet, 6E gives the *same* Wi-Fi 6 efficiency plus a private highway. Wi-Fi 7 (works on the same 6 GHz) pushes throughput higher with even wider channels and multi-link, but 6E is where the practical leap lives in 2026.

## Wi-Fi 6 realities in deployment

- **Backhaul still matters**: an AP fed by a 100 Mb/s Ethernet port delivering 1 Gb/s wireless is just a bottleneck on a stick. Plan port speed = peak wireless, not wireless speed.
- **Clients must be Wi-Fi 6 capable to benefit**: mixed fleets get only the improvement that the *oldest* client can use in that exchange (per-BSS-wide effects like BSS coloring and OFDMA help everyone, per-client features require new clients).
- **RF design is still RF design**: no protocol fixes a dead spot. Coverage survey, antenna placement, and channel planning are still the prerequisites — 6E just removes the neighbors-your-neighbors problem.
- **Monitor with the right metric**: look at *airtime utilization* and *retry rate*, not RSSI. A noisy airtime-90% AP is worse than a "slower" clean one.

## Tester checklist

- Compare AP throughput with a single Wi-Fi 6 client vs a device on Wi-Fi 5 mixed and explain the delta (expected: modest for one, big for many).
- Load the AP: hit it with 30-50 clients doing small IoT-style frames and confirm OFDMA keeps latency/airtime low (that's the design goal, not peak Mbps).
- Enable TWT on a low-power device and confirm battery drain drops measurably.
- If you have a 6 GHz-capable client, move it to 6 GHz and verify it leaves 2.4/5 GHz devices (and legacy 5 GHz DFS waits) alone.
- Verify BSS coloring actually kicks in on a dense apartment test: two APs on the same channel with clients should hurt less than pre-6.

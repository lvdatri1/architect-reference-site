## What this system is

A tenancy management system runs the **contract-and-cash side** of renting: who rents which unit, under what lease, at what rent, with which deposit — and what happens on move-in, renewal, and vacate. Where the [housing management system](../housing-management-systems) owns the *building*, the tenancy system owns the *stream of income*. The two meet at the unit, and the tenancy is what makes a unit an asset rather than a set of rooms.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  U[Unit] --> M[Marketing + screening]
  M --> L[Lease / tenancy agreement]
  L --> IN[Move-in + deposit]
  IN --> RC[Rent cycle - invoices and arrears]
  RC --> RN[Renewal / rent review]
  RN --> OU[Move-out - deposit return]
  OU --> RE[Re-let the unit]
```

## The core objects

| Object | Holds | Owned by | Failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tenancy / lease | Tenant, unit, term, rent, clauses | Tenancy system | Term ends but system keeps charging |
| Rent schedule | Due dates, amounts, payment splits | Tenancy system | Payment never allocates to the schedule |
| Deposit | Amount, protected?, refund state | Tenancy system | Refund paid but protection not closed out |
| Arrears / credit | What the tenant owes | Tenancy system | Doubled by an unfiled payment |
| Notice | Type, dates, expiry | Tenancy system | Expired notice auto-rolls the tenancy |

The tenancy is the **unit of billing**: everything financial hangs off the tenancy record, not the tenant alone (a tenant with two units gets two tenancies and two rent schedules). Mistakes here are money mistakes — doubled invoices, unfiled payments, deposits paid back without protection closure.

## The rental lifecycle

```mermaid
flowchart TB
  AV[Unit available] --> L1[Market + screen tenant]
  L1 --> L2[Sign lease - term + rent]
  L2 --> M1[Move-in - keys, inventory, deposit]
  M1 --> R1[Monthly rent cycle]
  R1 --> R2[Renewal or notice]
  R2 -->|renew| R1
  R2 -->|vacate| MO[Move-out - inspection]
  MO --> DR[Deposit return + final account]
  DR --> RE[Unit back to available]
```

## The rent cycle, close up

```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
  participant System
  participant Tenant
  participant Bank
  System->>Tenant: invoice for the month
  Tenant->>Bank: payment arrives
  Bank->>System: payment notification
  System->>System: allocate payment to rent schedule
  alt full and on time
    System-->>Tenant: receipt, ledger clean
  else partial or late
    System->>System: split + arrears accrues
    System-->>Tenant: warning + late fee per lease
  end
```

Rent accounting is a **cash-allocation problem**: a payment rarely matches exactly one invoice. The system must split it across schedule lines, apply late fees *in lease order*, and keep the running balance legible — because every tenant disputes the math at some point.

## The let → rent → vacate path

```mermaid
stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> Lease: sign
  Lease: Lease signed
  Lease --> MoveIn: handover
  MoveIn: Move-in + deposit
  MoveIn --> Rent: schedule starts
  Rent: Rent cycle
  Rent --> Renew: term end
  Renew: Renewal / notice
  Renew --> Vacate: move-out
  Vacate: Vacate + refund
  Vacate --> [*]
```

## Tester checklist

- Term end: a non-renewed tenancy must stop generating invoices on the exact end date and return the unit to available in the housing system.
- Arrears math: a partial payment must allocate to the oldest-falling rent line first and apply the late fee per the lease order — verify the running balance line by line.
- Deposit lifecycle: deposit protected on move-in must be closed out (protected → refunded/kept with reasons) on vacate — no orphan deposits.
- Notice logic: an expired notice must not auto-roll into a renewal without an explicit action.
- Re-let linking: vacating one tenancy must free the unit for the next while preserving history for both.

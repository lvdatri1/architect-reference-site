Core banking systems

The account-and-ledger backbone of a bank: who owns what money, how a transaction moves it, double-entry posting, and the difference between real-time and overnight processing.

Solution ArchitectData ArchitectEnterprise ArchitectBackendTester#banking#ledgers#finance#transaction-processing#architecture

What this system is

A core banking system is the bank's double-entry ledger plus the applications that read and write it: account and product definitions, deposits and loans, customer identification, interest, fees, and statutory posting. "Core" means exactly what it sounds like — everything else in the bank (cards, payments, treasury, reporting, CRM) is a satellite that moves money through the core or reconciles against it.

The core's job is brutal simplicity under pressure: for every transaction it must know who has the money, how much, and what the ledger master says after the post. A bank that cannot state its balances overnight is a bank in regulatory trouble by morning.

What makes it a ledger, not a CRUD app

ConceptMeaning in banking
Double-entryEvery post debits one account and credits another; the ledger always sums to zero
Account vs GLCustomer accounts live in subledgers; the finance GL rolls them up — see ERP finance record-to-report
Posting strategyOnline (real-time, before you reply) vs batch (end-of-day, historically)
Debits and creditsMoney in is a credit to your account; money out is a debit — two views of the same posting
Master filesReusable product definitions (interest rules, limits) so accounts are just instances
Transaction logEvery post is an immutable, numbered entry — the audit trail and the legal record

The identity of a transaction is the thing architects obsess over: a transaction reference, a date and time, a value date, an account, an amount, and the balancing post. Lose any of those and reconciliation breaks.

Online vs batch — the two computings

Traditionally, debit transactions posted online (the balance must drop the instant you pay) and credits were written at the end of day, because money arriving overnight needed reconciliation and interest to be computed. Modern cores post credit almost as fast, but the design split is still alive:

  • Real-time posting: funds move at the moment of the transaction; channel sees new balances instantly.
  • Overnight processing: interest, fees, statement runs, and statutory files happen in a batch window — the notorious "end of day", which is why banks still schedule maintenance windows around midnight.

The strategic question for an architect is: can our ledger post strictly, under high concurrency, while our batch still runs to a hard deadline? The batch window is usually the bottleneck that forces separate read and write paths.

When this shows up in architecture

  • Modernization: replacing a 40-year-old core is a multi-year program; the usual path is strangler — new channel APIs in front, a payments hub beside it, and the legacy core as downstream ledger until the runway closes.
  • Multi-entity: group banks need a shared or federated ledger per legal entity, with consolidation for group reporting.
  • Digital banking: the mobile app must feel real-time even when the core is herd-batch — ledger reads from a read replica or an operational data store, writes still hit the iron core.

Tester checklist

  • Atomicity: send $100 from A to B with B's account temporarily frozen — confirm the pair either fully posts or fully rolls back, never half-applied.
  • Double-entry invariant: after a run of mixed deposits, withdrawals, fees, and reversals, the ledger sums to zero with no unexplained entries.
  • Idempotency: re-send an identical transaction reference twice — it must post once and return the same reference.
  • Concurrency: two payments emptying the same account cannot make the balance go negative past the agreed limit.
  • Value date: a transaction posted today with tomorrow's value date lands on the correct GL day, not the posting day.
  • Reversal: reversing a posted entry produces offsetting entries and restores the account and the GL to the pre-transaction state.
  • Batch deadline: the overnight run completes within the window even with a padded transaction volume.

## What this system is

**Record-to-report (R2R)** is the ERP chain that turns business events into financial statements. Every operational post — a sale, a purchase, a shipment, a payment, a return — eventually becomes a **journal entry** against the **general ledger (GL)**. Finance modules live downstream of everything: [order-to-cash](../enterprise-resource-planning) feeds revenue, source-to-pay feeds liabilities, and inventory movements feed COGS (cost of goods sold).

The GL is deliberately simple: accounts, debits and credits, a period (month), and a ledger. The sophistication is in what *writes* to it. When an architect says "ERP finance", they mostly mean the plumbing that keeps every upstream module's postings balanced and reconcilable.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    OT[Order-to-cash<br/>revenue + AR] --> GL[General ledger]
    SP[Source-to-pay<br/>liabilities + AP] --> GL
    PM[Plan-to-produce<br/>inventory + COGS] --> GL
    HR[Hire-to-retire<br/>payroll + accruals] --> GL
    GL --> P[Period close<br/>adjustments + consolidation]
    P --> R[Financial reports<br/>P&L, balance sheet, cash flow]
```

## The double-entry discipline

Every posting is a **balanced** set of debits and credits, grouped by account, department, project, and profit center. A single goods receipt touches several dims, so the finance module's job is to keep the *slices* (accounting view) consistent with the *totals* (the ledger view). Where this breaks is the eternal arguments about **valuation**:

| Method | What it costs when | Surprise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard costing | A planned unit cost; differences record as variances | Variances pile up when prices drift |
| Moving average | Every receipt re-averages the unit cost | Fine until an audit wants exact layers |
| FIFO / LIFO | Costs attach to specific receipt layers | Order of layers becomes the tax shape |

The choice matters to the GL but is mostly invisible to operations — except at **period close**, when the variance accounts must be cleared and explained.

## A whole chain landing in one entry

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  Event["Business event<br/>ship goods / pay invoice"] -->|debit side| Dr["Dr: COGS / AP<br/>Dr: asset or expense"]
  Event -->|credit side| Cr["Cr: inventory / AR<br/>Cr: revenue / liability"]
  Dr -->|sum to zero| GL["GL posting<br/>balancing, dated, referenced"]
  Cr -->|sum to zero| GL
  GL -->|post| Book["Ledger + subledgers<br/>AR, AP, assets"]
  Book -->|close + report| Rep["Period close + reports"]
```

## Period close, the monthly ritual

Close has four jobs, in this order — and each one either passes or blocks the month:

1. **Reconcile subledgers to GL** — AR and AP balances match the top-level accounts; mismatches halt the close.
2. **Run valuations and adjustments** — inventory valuation, accruals, depreciation, currency revaluation.
3. **Freeze and post** — lock prior periods; post only to the open period.
4. **Report and consolidate** — P&L, balance sheet, cash flow, for each entity and the group.

The metric that matters is **time-to-close**: a modern ERP posts continuously so the close is confirmation, not catch-up. If your close still needs a week of spreadsheets, the subledgers and the GL have silently diverged all month.

## The reconciliation truth

The GL is the last word for reporting, and it will only ever be as accurate as the feeds that reach it. The classic failure is the **settlement loop**: the payment gateway clears money in batches, the [e-commerce platform](../ecommerce-platform-architecture) records orders, and the ERP posts revenue — and the three never tie out line-by-line. The finance architecture that survives an audit automates three-way matching on *every* posting, not just the big ones.

## Tester checklist

- Balancing: introduce a malformed feed (an unbalanced or missing dimension posting) and confirm it is rejected, never split silently into the GL.
- Subledger to GL: run the reconciliation for a month with known AP/AR transactions — zero unexplained difference.
- Valuation switch: recalculate COGS under standard and moving-average for the same inventory, and confirm the GL variance accounts carry the difference explainably.
- Close order: attempt to post to a closed period and confirm it is blocked; post an adjustment to the open period and confirm the prior period's reports are unchanged.
- Feed integrity: guarantee even delivery of every source-systems feed (retry, dedupe, idempotency) so a re-sent posting cannot double-count.
- Report drill-down: every report figure bisects to journal entries, source documents, and original events.

Network access control (NAC)

The gatekeeper that checks a device's identity and posture before it gets network access — 802.1X, posture assessment, and the quarantine loop.

Network EngineerSecurity ArchitectEndpoint Device EngineerCloud Engineer#network#security#endpoint#architecture

What this system is

Network Access Control (NAC) is the gate at the edge of the network: before a device is allowed onto the LAN, Wi-Fi, or VPN, NAC checks what it is (identity) and how healthy it is (posture), then grants access, restricts it, or quarantines it. It's the network-layer enforcement arm of endpoint security and zero trust: the old "it's plugged into our switch, so it's trusted" assumption is replaced with "prove identity and posture first." Think of NAC as 802.1X on the wire, plus a health check, plus a quarantine loop.

The three layers of NAC

LayerWhat it doesThe failure if it's missing
802.1X authenticationPort-based auth via RADIUS before the port/AP gives accessAny physical jack is a trusted port; unmanaged devices get straight in
Posture assessmentChecks AV present/current, patches, policy compliance after authA valid identity (even stolen) with a compromised device passes
Enforcement / quarantineDrops non-compliant devices onto a remediation VLAN until fixedNon-compliant devices sit on the trusted LAN indefinitely

The mental model: identity gets you onto the wire; posture gets you into the trusted zone. NAC is the mechanism that makes "prove it before you're inside" literal at layer 2 — a sick or unknown device is contained to a guest/remediation network, not just "flagged" on a dashboard.

The key modern shift: identity is the primary key, posture is the secondary check. NAC used to be "can this MAC address in?" — now it's "is this a real, enrolled, attested device with a current posture?" That's why NAC is tightly coupled to MDM and endpoint enrollment: the posture data has to come from a trusted source on the device, not from what the device claims about itself.

NAC vs the things it gets confused with

ThingWhat it actually isVersus NAC
802.1XThe protocol/port-auth mechanism (EAP + RADIUS)NAC's authentication layer, not the whole thing
Firewall / microsegmentationLayer 3–7 filtering between zonesNAC gates the entry edge; firewalls gate traffic after entry
MAC filteringAllow-list of MAC addressesTrivial to spoof; NAC adds real identity + posture on top
SASE / ZTNACloud edge access control for remote/cloudThe same idea, moved off-LAN; NAC is the on-prem LAN version

The strategic read: NAC secures the physical LAN edge; SASE / ZTNA secures the remote-and-cloud edge. Modern estates don't choose one — they get NAC for the office floor and SASE for the rest of the world, with the same identity/posture logic underneath.

Why NAC projects disappoint (and what actually works)

DisappointmentThe real causeThe fix
"We bought NAC and nothing happened"Enforcement never turned on; it was deployed observe-onlyDecide the quarantine policy before rollout; enforce on a pilot VLAN first
Posture checks trusted the device's claimAgent/device report not validated against a trusted sourceRequire an enrolled endpoint / MDM attestation, not a self-report
Real devices got quarantinedStale posture source (old AV definition list, expired certs)Revalidate posture data, not assumptions; allow remediation to be fast and self-service
Guests / printers / IoT brokeNetwork assumed all devices are managedNon-managed categories get explicit guest/IoT policies, not a silent default-deny
Quarantine loop was a dead endDevices stuck in quarantine with no path backA remediation portal the user actually sees, with one-click patch/update flows

The pattern that works: NAC as a hygiene gate with a fast remediation loop, not as an access-control barricade. Enforce on a small-but-real VLAN, measure how often genuine users hit quarantine, and make "fix it and come back" a 5-minute path rather than a ticket.

The failure modes an architect owns

  • Enforcement never enabled — the classic: NAC deployed in monitor-only mode forever, so it reports but never blocks.
  • Trusting the device's own claim — no enrolled endpoint identity, so a compromised-but-"healthy"-claiming device walks straight through.
  • Stale posture sources — checking against an AV definition list nobody updates; posture checks confirm yesterday's truth.
  • One-size quarantine — printers, HVAC, and unmanaged guests all dumped into a "quarantine" with no path back or wrong per-category policy.
  • No BYOD story — personal devices with no agent get default-deny or default-trust, instead of a designed guest/remediation posture.
  • NAC for the LAN only — remote/cloud devices are invisible to the gate, so the office is protected and everything else isn't (hence SASE).

Tester checklist

  • Identity gate: connect a device with no/unknown identity and confirm it lands on guest/quarantine, not the trusted VLAN.
  • Posture gate: connect a known identity with a deliberately stale AV/OS and confirm access is downgraded to quarantine with a visible remediation path.
  • Enforcement reality: confirm quarantine is enforced (traffic actually blocked), not merely logged — run a probe from a quarantined port.
  • Remediation loop: take a quarantined device, apply the fix, and confirm it re-authenticates into the trusted VLAN within the target time, self-service.
  • Posture-source truth: break the posture source (expire the AV definition list, kill the MDM attestation) and confirm devices are not auto-granted on stale data.
  • Guest/IoT segregation: connect a printer/guest and confirm they get the designed IoT/guest policy, never the trusted employee VLAN.

## What this system is

A **grant management system (GMS)** runs the life-cycle of money given by one organization (a federal agency, state, or foundation) to another (a state, university, non-profit, or local government) to carry out a program. Unlike [procurement (source-to-pay)](../procurement-source-to-pay), a grant is *not* a purchase of goods or services — it is the **funding of an outcome**, with the rules for cost allowability, performance, and reporting attached at the moment of award.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    NOFO[Opportunity announced<br/>NOFO / FOA] --> APP[Application + merit review]
    APP --> AW[Award - terms + budget]
    AW --> DD[Drawdown - spending]
    DD --> REP[Performance + financial reporting]
    REP --> CL[Closeout - certify + close]
    CL -.->|lessons| NOFO
```

## The six phases

| Phase | What happens | The artifact that decides it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 · Opportunity** | Agency publishes a funding opportunity (NOFO/FOA) with eligibility, priorities, and evaluation criteria | The announcement is the contract-with-the-applicants |
| **2 · Apply** | Applicants submit a full application: narrative, budget, team, data | The proposal — evaluated against announcement criteria |
| **3 · Review** | Merit review by objective technical reviewers scores against the published criteria | Scores + written critiques, in rank order |
| **4 · Award** | Top-ranked applications get an award with budget, period of performance, and terms & conditions | The award notice — the rules and the money |
| **5 · Operate** | Recipient draws down funds, spends to the budget, reports performance and finances | Progress + financial reports feeding recipient monitoring |
| **6 · Closeout** | Work and spend certified, final reports in, unspent balance returned | The closeout certification |

The defining feature versus a purchase: **nobody "pays" for the performance; the awardee is trusted to spend toward the goal, and the grant is only as good as the monitoring and reporting she can prove.**  That reporting *is* the product of a GMS — not the award record.

## The award-to-drawdown flow

```mermaid
stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> App: submit
  App: Application - narrative + budget
  App --> Review: submit
  Review: Merit review - scored, evidenced
  Review --> Award: highest ranked
  Award: Award notice - terms + period
  Award --> Draw: approved
  Draw: Drawdown request
  Draw --> Funds: disburse
  Funds: Funds disbursed
  Funds --> Report: account for spend + results
  Report: Progress + financial reports back
  Report --> [*]
```

## The compliance obligations that make GMS distinct

- **Cost allowability** — every dollar must be *allowed* (necessary, reasonable, allocable, consistent): personnel, travel, equipment, indirect. `Unallowable` costs are disallowed at audit.
- **Indirect cost rates** — recipients charge overhead via a negotiated indirect rate; getting the negotiated-rate model wrong is the classic source of year-end disallowances.
- **Drawdown rules** — for the big federal systems, funds are drawn via the Payment Management System (PMS) *as needed*, not paid up-front as a lump sum, and drawdown is tied to actual cash need.
- **Reporting on a calendar** — standard data elements for performance reporting (and the administrative/data standards that gave us the modern tech platforms) mean reports are structured, machine-readable records — a genuine [data-modeling](../data-modeling-star-schemas) problem, not free text.
- **Audit at the top** — recipients are audited, so the GMS must make *evidence traceable*: budget to drawdown to expenditure to report, per award, per period.

## When you'll meet a GMS as an architect

- **Agency side** (the funder): the GMS runs opportunity → review → award → monitoring, publishes notices, and feeds portfolio analytics. It is often *not* an ERP because grants have their own life-cycle and their own [finance/reporting](../erp-finance-and-record-to-report) rules.
- **Recipient side** (university, state government, non-profit): the GMS manages the award book, pre-award budgets, effort allocation, and the drawdown-and-report loop back to the funder. This side lives in the gap between the [HR effort systems](../hr-and-talent-systems) (who works on what) and the general ledger.
- **The seam**: both sides exchange structured award and report data; the best GMS purchases win on *data exchange quality* (standard schema, API, reliable feeds) not on the glamour screens.

## Tester checklist

- Application review integrity: an application is scored only against its announcement's criteria; a score change requires an audit trail with dated reason.
- Award consistency: the award record, the budget, and the notice agree line-for-line, and the budget balances to the total award.
- Drawdown controls: a drawdown in excess of budget or unreleated to cash need is blocked or flagged, and every drawdown is traceable to the award.
- Spend-to-report: month-end financial reports reconcile the budget-to-actual per cost category, and disallowed costs are visible at report time, not audit time.
- Period-of-performance enforcement: spending and reporting outside the award's period (including extensions) are prevented or require an approved amendment.
- Closeout: only awards with all reports in and under-spend resolved can be closed, and the closeout is recorded as the final evidence.

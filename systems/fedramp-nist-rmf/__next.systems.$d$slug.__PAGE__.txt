## What this system is

The **Risk Management Framework (RMF)** is the process by which a US federal system gets permission to operate: choose a baseline of **NIST SP 800-53 controls**, implement and assess them, document the residual risk, and obtain an **Authority to Operate (ATO)** from an Authorizing Official. **FedRAMP** is the program that applies the same discipline to *cloud services* — one high-fidelity assessment that agencies all reuse instead of each re-auditing the same provider.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    CAT[Categorize<br/>impact level] --> SEL[Select controls<br/>800-53 baseline]
    SEL --> IMP[Implement + document]
    IMP --> ASS[Assess - test the controls]
    ASS --> AUTH[Authorize - ATO decision]
    AUTH --> MON[Monitor - continuous monitoring]
    MON -.->|changes| IMP
```

## Impact levels first, everything else follows

Every control is scoped by the **impact level** of the data — the harm that loss of confidentiality, integrity, or availability would cause:

| Level | Data example | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Low** | Public-ish, unclassified operational data | Smallest control baseline |
| **Moderate** | PII, sensitive operational data | Larger baseline, more documented procedures |
| **High** | High-risk data, law-enforcement sensitive, serious injury/life-focused | Full baseline + extra hardening, often dedicated infrastructure |

In **FedRAMP** those map to **FedRAMP Low, Moderate, High** authorizations; an agency can only run a workload on a cloud service authorized at least as high as the data it will hold. The classification and the authorization *must match*, or the deployment is noncompliant from day one.

## The control families, the real workload

SP 800-53 organizes ~400+ controls into **18 families**. The ones that dominate real assessments:

| Family | What it governs | The controls that eat the most time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AC · Access Control** | Who can touch what | Least privilege, separation of duties, session mgmt |
| **IA · Identification & Authentication** | Proving identity | Multi-factor, authenticator mgmt, and the [PIV/CAC](../government-identity-piv-ficam) linkage |
| **AU · Audit & Accountability** | Logging, evidence | Audit records, review/analysis, retention — the proof trail |
| **SC · System & Communications** | Cryptographic protection | Encryption in transit/at rest, boundary protection |
| **SI · System & Information Integrity** | Patching, malware, failure | Continuous monitoring, flaw remediation |
| **CP · Contingency** | Survival | Backup, recovery, failover testing |

Controls are not a checklist you print once — each one is a **system control plus a documented, assessed procedure**, and for cloud the [shared responsibility](../cloud-computing-fundamentals) split decides who supplies what.

## The authorization decision, step by step

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    DATA[Categorize - data impact] -- drives scope --> BASE[Select baseline - 800-53 Low/Mod/High]
    BASE -- tailor --> IMPL[Implement - SPD + procedures]
    IMPL -- evidence --> ASSESS[Assess - independent testing]
    ASSESS -- findings + POA&M --> PACK[Package - POA&M + residual risk]
    PACK -- authorizing official --> ATO[ATO - go or no-go]
```

The **ATO is a business decision, not a test pass**: the Authorizing Official signs on *residual risk* — the findings in the Plan of Action & Milestones (POA&M), the mitigating controls, and the acceptance that the system is good enough given its mission. Systems never wait for "zero findings"; they wait for *informed* acceptance, then run on **continuous monitoring** (CLOUD for cloud: continuous logs, ongoing vulnerability scanning, and periodic reassessment) until the authorization is re-upped or the risk picture changes.

## How FedRAMP changes the cloud play

- **FedRAMP High = one assessment, many agencies.** A cloud service provider (CSP) does the huge assessment once against the full baseline; agencies do a small *inheritance* check and a [cloud landing zone](../cloud-landing-zones) onboarding, then issue their own ATO scoped to the CSP's boundary.
- **Inheritance**, not reinvention: controls owned by the CSP (physical security, platform patching) are *inherited* by the tenant once the CSP is authorized; the tenant still owns app-layer controls. The shared responsibility split is a security-control mapping decision.
- **FedRAMP High vs Moderate** shapes architecture: encryption of data at rest everywhere, FedRAMP-High-authorized regional infrastructure, and — most visibly — it usually *excludes* cheap public-region tenants without additional compensation and the same assurances.

## How it shows up for an architect

- **Before you build**: categorize the data, pick the baseline, and buy cloud only at the right authorization level — then the control set, not the feature list, drives the design.
- **During build**: audit/audit/audit — logging (AU), boundary (SC-7), and authenticators (IA) are where most assessment findings concentrate, so build them in, instrument them, and make evidence exportable.
- **At the end**: the ATO is in the *package* — System Security Plan (SSP), control implementation details, assessment results, POA&M, and the risk acceptance narrative.

## Tester checklist

- Categorization match: confirm the implemented controls and infrastructure at least meet the declared impact level and FedRAMP authorization of the data and cloud service.
- Control-to-evidence: for a sample of controls (one AC, one IA, one AU), the implementation description is backed by a testable, repeatable procedure.
- Boundary completeness (SC-7): only documented interfaces traverse the boundary; no undocumented data egress paths exist in the network design.
- Audit coverage: every system component and every privileged action produces records that are aggregated, retained to policy, and reviewable.
- POA&M closure: each open finding has an owner, a due date, and a compensating control in place pending remediation.
- Inheritance correct: the split of CSP-owned vs tenant-owned controls matches the real deployed architecture, not the marketing boundary diagram.

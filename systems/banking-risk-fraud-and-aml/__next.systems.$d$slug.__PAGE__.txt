## What this system is

Banks must not only transact — they must **know their customer is who they claim, their money is not laundered, and their losses are survivable**. That is the risk and compliance layer: a set of checks that sit *alongside* the [core ledger](../core-banking-systems) and [payment rails](../payments-clearing-and-settlement), screening risk, fraud, and money-laundering on every transaction, and reporting the results to regulators.

It is genuinely a second system behind the account ledger: while account balance is the primary record, the *risk posture* of a customer, a transaction, and the bank itself is what gets examined under audit.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    TX[Transaction arrives] --> FRAUD[Fraud detection - real-time scoring]
    FRAUD --> F[Case review when flagged]
    FRAUD --> KYC[KYC / AML checks - errors, sanctions]
    KYC --> R[Reporting - STR, SAR, regulatory filings]
    FRAUD --> RISK[Risk limits - credit, exposure, liquidity]
    RISK --> TX2[Approved / declined / referred]
```

## The three control layers

| Control | Question it answers | Latency required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Credit & market risk** | Is the exposure, the counterparty, or the market move beyond appetite? | Near-real-time, plus batch |
| **Fraud detection** | Is this *the rightful owner* making this transaction from this device/path? | Milliseconds (real-time) |
| **AML / KYC** | Is this customer/sender/beneficiary sanctioned, PEP, or patterned like laundering? | Seconds on transaction, broader on onboarding |

The trap is mixing them: a **credit** decision ("can we afford this counterparty") is different from a **fraud** decision ("is this really the customer"), and conning them together either rejects good business or lets bad through. Architects should keep the data model *one funnel with several scorers*, not one scary "risk flag".

## Real-time fraud, step by step

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    TX["Transaction event"] -->|raw| ENRICH["Enrich<br/>device, geo, velocity, patterns"]
    ENRICH -->|features| MODEL["ML + rules engine<br/>score the action"]
    MODEL -->|pass| LOW["Low risk - auto pass"]
    MODEL -->|friction| MID["Medium - step-up<br/>2FA, re-verification"]
    MODEL -->|deny| HIGH["High - block + review"]
```

The design tension is **customer friction vs false positives**. A step-up challenge (OTP, biometric, or re-login) stops fraud cheaply and is reversible; a hard decline is not. Good fraud systems are calibrated to push *uncertainty* toward step-up rather than blanket decline, and the calibration is a business decision owned jointly with the risk team.

## KYC and AML: the know-your-customer loop

- **Onboarding**: identity verification (document + liveness), PEP/sanctions screening, and risk-rating at account opening.
- **Ongoing monitoring**: transaction monitoring looks for structuring, rapid movement, and inconsistent profiles; sanctions lists are refreshed continuously.
- **Reporting**: suspicions become **STR/SAR** (suspicious transaction/activity reports) — a regulator-required, confidential filing that *must not* tip off the customer.
- **The net effect**: an AML program is a *filter that produces regulated outputs*, so the boring engineering — reliable feeds, dedupe, auditable history, and case-management — matters more than any single scorer.

## Risk appetite: the bank's own threshold

Risk controls are only meaningful against an explicit appetite: limits per customer, per sector, per counterparty, and per product. The architecture must let treasury/credit **set limits in one place** and have every channel and every payment rail read them — the same refusal should show in the API, the branch, and the wire room. Disjointed limit tables are how a bank discovers its exposure was really double what any one system reported.

## Tester checklist

- Fraud threshold: craft the same transaction from warm, stepped-up, and anomalous contexts — each routes to the intended outcome (auto-pass, step-up, block).
- Friction rule: a medium-risk event triggers step-up exactly once per session, not a retry loop that abandons the customer.
- AML screening: a counterparty on a sanctions list is blocked at onboarding *and* at payment time, and the block generates the required case record.
- Limited exposure: a customer limit raised in treasury is reflected in the wire, card, and API paths within the SLA, not per-system lazily.
- STR handling: a suspicious transaction produces a report that is fully audited and *never* leaks to the customer-facing view.
- Idempotency of monitoring: replays of a transaction feed do not double-count the monitoring signal or generate duplicate cases.
- Calibration evidence: every pass/step-up/deny decision can be re-derived from its features for the regulator, with an auditable model version.
